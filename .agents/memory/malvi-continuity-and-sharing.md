---
name: Malvi continuity and sharing
description: User expectations for returning conversations, camera consent and evidence-based internet research.
---
Malvi should recognize returning users by their authenticated account and ask
whether to continue the previous discussion or start something new, rather
than repeating her introduction.

**Why:** The user explicitly asked for continuity across later Malvi invitations
and recall of what that user previously discussed.

**How to apply:** Keep account-private long-term transcripts separate from
unowned browser history. Give users visible consent, inspection and deletion
controls. An archive is not unlimited model context or a guarantee of perfect
recall; distinguish unavailable history from an empty history.

Camera sharing must be explicitly initiated and permitted by the user, not
always-on observation.

**Why:** The user requested one-click/tap permission to show Malvi themselves,
objects, colours and their surroundings for discussion.

**How to apply:** Preserve visible permission and stop controls. Describe
observable details, not inferred identity, health or emotional state. Do not
claim image non-retention by an external provider merely because Nanivio does
not save the image.

Internet and social-media research should provide attributable evidence,
not an assurance of infallibility or private social-account access.

**Why:** The user wants accurate, valid information from internet research.

**How to apply:** Show actual returned citations and evidence limits, distinguish
social claims from corroboration, and keep private conversation/account data
out of public search queries. Provider configuration alone is not a successful
live research test.

Driving navigation is handed off to Google Maps after explicit user action;
Malvi should not provide invented live routes or turn-by-turn guidance.

**Why:** The user selected “Open Google Maps for navigation (recommended)”
when choosing how Malvi should provide route assistance.

**How to apply:** Use an explicit destination and user-supplied origin or
permissioned GPS. Label IP geolocation as approximate, never use it for a
driving origin, and do not obtain location automatically. The user also wants
separate copyable text answers and visual media with source links.
